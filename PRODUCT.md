# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two primary audiences visit a Chip Zenix surface:

- **Technical decision-makers at semiconductor / electronics companies** (VP Engineering,
  design managers, founders/CTOs) evaluating an engineering-services partner for VLSI/ASIC or
  embedded work. They arrive skeptical, scanning for proof of real capability and dependability,
  often under schedule pressure. The job to be done: decide quickly whether these people can be
  trusted with a hard, expensive project — then start a conversation.
- **Engineers considering joining** (VLSI/embedded talent). They read the same site for
  credibility and culture; an authentic technical voice is what converts them.

Secondary: partners / investors performing due-diligence-grade scrutiny of legitimacy and reach.

## Product Purpose

A marketing/brand website for Chip Zenix, a semiconductor engineering-services company
(VLSI & ASIC design, embedded systems, AI hardware acceleration). It exists to convert qualified
B2B interest into a contact (via WhatsApp), establish credibility for investors/partners, attract
engineering talent, and serve as a world-class brand presence. Success = a skeptical technical
visitor leaves convinced Chip Zenix is expert and dependable, and reaches out.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **precise · authoritative · ambitious.** Voice is exact, confident, technically
literate — an expert who has nothing to prove and therefore doesn't oversell. Bold through clarity
and scale, never through hype or noise. Emotional goal: the calm confidence of handing a hard
problem to people who clearly know more than you do. Physical-object reference: a precision
instrument — a machined chassis, an oscilloscope readout, a clean datasheet — not a brochure.

## Anti-references

Explicitly NOT:
- **Generic SaaS landing template** — gradient blobs, identical feature-card grids, the
  big-number hero cliché. The default AI look.
- **Dense legacy-corporate / consultancy** — cramped, text-heavy, brochure density.
- **Flashy gamer / neon-RGB** — aggressive effects, glows everywhere, toy-like.
- **Cheap / free-theme** — anything that erodes B2B + investor trust.
- **Generic dark "dev-tool/terminal" look** — the second-order reflex for a chip company; dark is
  justified here only by the brand's own committed identity (the logo), executed as precision
  instrumentation, not as a costume terminal.
- **Editorial-magazine affectation** — display-serif + italic + ruled-column layout; wrong register.

## Design Principles

1. **Precision is the proof.** Every detail is exact — alignment, rhythm, timing. Sloppiness on a
   semiconductor site reads as bad engineering. Craft is the argument.
2. **Show capability, don't claim it.** Concrete specs, real flows, tangible detail over adjectives.
   An engineer trusts evidence, not marketing.
3. **Confident restraint.** One decisive idea per fold; bold via scale and clarity, never via noise.
   The dark field stays calm so the cyan signal means something.
4. **Engineer-credible voice.** Authentic technical language; mono used only for real data/specs
   (where it's true to the domain), never as decoration.
5. **Trust is the conversion.** Every section ladders toward "these people are serious and
   dependable, and contacting them is low-risk."

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥ 4.5:1, large/UI text ≥ 3:1, placeholders ≥ 4.5:1. Fully keyboard
navigable with visible focus; semantic landmarks; labeled form fields with accessible error
messaging. Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` fallback (including the
horizontal-scroll sections). Hidden scrollbar must never break keyboard/touch scrolling.
